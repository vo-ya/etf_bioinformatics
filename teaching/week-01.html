Teaching week 1

Ask students what they would measure to investigate slower cell growth. Introduce DNA, RNA, and proteins through the fictional cultures, then follow a DNA fragment into a sequencing file. Homework compares the estimated errors of synthetic reads.

Open companion presentationDownload homework solutions

Class and homework are separate

The class lasts 180 minutes, including a ten-minute break, short demonstrations, and group discussion. Students need no prior biology and do no required programming during class. Homework 01 follows the class, takes approximately 60–90 minutes, and assumes basic Python strings, lists, loops, and functions. The optional extension takes additional time.

Running the activities

Use the activity timings in the schedule above. Ask pairs for a prediction before changing a control. For a projected demonstration, collect predictions with a show of hands.

  • Cell response: choose an explanation, read the feedback, then explain why DNA can stay the same while RNA abundance changes.
  • Signal experiment: ask when a competing channel will win. At 60 the top channels tie; above 60 the simple rule calls C although the simulated base is A. The gap is not a Phred score.
  • Measurement chooser: hold the measurement fixed and change the question. Discuss why its usefulness changes and what further evidence is needed.
  • Read retention: compare 8, 6, 4, and 0 retained bases. Mean estimated error probabilities are 2.755%, 0.34%, 0.01%, and undefined respectively. More confident retained calls can come at the cost of less usable sequence.

Introducing sequencing on slide 11

Start with the DNA question raised on slide 10. Define sequencing as determining base order, then reveal input, measurement, and output. Ask students to distinguish a physical DNA fragment from a reported read. Noise can alter an inferred base without changing the original DNA. This prediction is tested in the nanopore activity. The platform comparison follows preparation and precedes the nanopore close-up.

Library preparation and paired reads / 5 minutes

Use the library and read-pair activity after the sequencing introduction in the 60–95 block. Spend two minutes tracing the insert and added adapters, one minute explaining the opposite mate orientations and possible unread middle, and two minutes on the origin-counting prompt.

Answer: if three PCR copies of one starting fragment each yield one read pair, there are six read records, one original fragment, and one source culture; no new independent cultures were created. The mates share a fragment and the copy-derived pairs share an original molecule. These are repeated technical observations of one fragment. Independent cultures are needed to assess variation between conditions.

Read-through: sequencing can continue beyond a short sample insert into the adapter on its opposite end. High base-call confidence does not make that adapter part of the genomic insert. The quality-trimming homework is not an adapter-recognition algorithm. Paired ends: both mate outputs are written 5′→3′ in opposite orientations along the fragment. With a 500-base insert and two 150-base reads, 200 middle bases are not directly read; the two records do not guarantee one continuous 300-base sequence. Mates can overlap for shorter inserts. Keep PCR optional and avoid kit-specific adapter memorization.

Translator, nanopore, and FASTQ answers

Translator (10 minutes): begin with coding DNA ATGGCTTTCTAA, frame +1. Students predict RNA AUG GCU UUC UAA, then step through methionine–alanine–phenylalanine followed by STOP. Changing GCT to GCC preserves the amino-acid sequence in the standard code. ATGTAAGCTTTC stops after methionine. The given frame and coding strand are assumptions; this is not a gene-finding or expression simulator. Ask students to use the genetic-code table to explain why GCU and GCC both encode alanine.

Nanopore simulation (10 minutes): give pairs two minutes to predict, four to explore sequence and noise controls, and four to compare electrical versus optical measurement. A repeated context can make the toy trace less distinctive; real pore chemistry, speed, and base-calling models are more complex. Before interpreting a trace, point out that the current levels are invented and are not calibrated instrument measurements.

FASTQ inspection (5 minutes): the inspector rejects mismatched sequence/quality lengths. After students restore the deleted character, ask why that success establishes only file consistency. The quality line can contain @ or + without becoming a new header or separator.

Platform comparison on slide 13

Compare signal, read length, and inference using the platform explanation and primary sources. Ask whether one read could bridge a 5 kb repeat: a sufficiently long Nanopore or HiFi read could connect unique flanks, while one 150-base read cannot. HiFi combines repeated passes over one insert; it does not create independent biological samples. Keep chemistry details and performance specifications outside the required outcomes.

Separate the cultures from the synthetic reads

The slower-growth observation is a fictional teaching premise. Read 1 and Read 2 are independent synthetic observations, not mates or measurements of Tube A and Tube B. The notebook’s 200 reads are also synthetic; use them only to compare measurement quality and trimming.

Opening: accept several possible explanations, including changed gene activity, cell state, or differences in handling. Ask what measurement could distinguish them. Ask how independently grown cultures in each condition would help assess biological variation, and whether preparation batch could differ with condition.

Shared DNA, different activity: regulation can change RNA abundance and cellular behaviour while DNA sequence remains essentially shared. RNA abundance does not by itself determine protein abundance or activity.

Ambiguous read: a sequence wholly within an identical repeat can match several places. Longer sequence, a mate, or other context may help. Connect this to the cost of trimming later.

Read prediction: both sequences are ACGTACGT. Read 1 has IIIIIIII (Q40 throughout); Read 2 has IIII55++ (Q40×4, Q20×2, Q10×2). Read 1 has lower estimated error probability where the scores differ. The final two calls in Read 2 have the highest estimated error probability. The scores estimate call-error probabilities; identifying actual errors requires a known sequence.

Exit answers

  1. DNA carries genetic information. Transcription produces RNA, some of which functions directly. Translation reads mRNA to assemble a protein. Measurements of these molecules serve different questions.
  2. A genome is an organism’s genetic material. A read is one reported sequence from a sequencing measurement, often covering only part of the genome.
  3. Q30 expresses an estimated base-call error probability of 0.001. A condition comparison also needs correct sample labels and independent cultures. A base-call score addresses only the estimated correctness of the letter.

Homework handoff and support

Ask students to complete Homework 01 after class and before week 2. Begin with their Read 1 / Read 2 prediction, then extend the same confidence question to 200 synthetic reads. The notebook includes Colab upload, save, run, and download instructions, a deterministic synthetic dataset, scaffolded code, and expandable hints. Its approximately 50 minutes of coding tasks sit within a 60–90-minute allowance for setup, debugging, and interpretation.

Submission is a completed notebook containing a positional quality plot or printed table, a before/after comparison, and three sentences covering the change, lost information, and limits. Accept the table if matplotlib is unavailable. A prepared local Jupyter environment can run the same assignment offline.

At the start of week 2, use a brief review of common misconceptions and one retention/uncertainty comparison. If students cannot finish the parser independently, use the hints and record this as feedback about scaffolding and workload.

Review criteria

After class, check students’ explanations: can they describe how RNA abundance can change without a DNA change? Can they distinguish six read records from one original fragment? Can they use the quality scores to compare the toy reads? Note where explanations break down during the 45-minute biology segment. Review homework completion time separately from class pacing.
