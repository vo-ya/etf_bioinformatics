Teaching Week 3

Students join overlapping fragments, construct a de Bruijn graph, and compare candidate reconstructions. Use the repeat and branch examples to ask which additional observations would distinguish them.

Open presentationDownload homework solutions

Preparation and scope

The class lasts 180 minutes, including a ten-minute break. Colab homework takes a separate 60–90 minutes. Students should be comfortable with reads, uncertainty, exact matching, and the idea that an optimal result depends on stated rules. No prior graph-theory course is required; introduce nodes, directed edges, paths, and branches visually.

The class uses invented short sequences. Our overlap model assumes exact letters in a shared forward orientation. The graph uses (k−1)-mer nodes and distinct k-mer edges with observed support counts. The traversal exercise permits each distinct edge once, suitable for its restricted examples. Read-observation counts describe support; converting them to genomic occurrence multiplicities requires a sampling model. Teach the graph representation and supplied paths here; leave strand-aware assembly and consensus algorithms for later study.

Open the three adapted tools before teaching. They work locally without downloads. Each iframe also has a full-tab link; use that for projection. Ask for predictions before changing controls. Keep the worked explanations available for groups without devices.

Worked checkpoints in the 40-slide deck

Slide 5 — Count depth and breadth
Starts [0,2,6], L=4, G=12 give depths 1,1,2,2,1,1,1,1,1,1,0,0. Sum 12 gives mean 1×; ten nonzero positions give breadth 83.3%. Including only covered positions changes the mean to 1.2×.
Slide 9 — Full breadth, missing order
ATGA, CCTG, GTAC cover the known target end to end but no read crosses either boundary. Without known starts, both displayed orders fit. GACC would span the ATGA/CCTG boundary; no pair has an overlap of at least three bases.
Slide 15 — Both candidates contain every read
In ATGATGAC, ATGA starts at 0 and 3, TGAC at 4, TGAT at 1. In ATGACATGAT, ATGA starts at 0 and 5, TGAC at 1, TGAT at 6. The shorter candidate is preferred by a length-minimizing rule, not established as biological truth.
Slides 17, 20, and 22 — Boundaries, objects, and edges
Concatenating the read records invents ACG and CGA; count six observed windows. Whole reads are overlap-graph nodes, whereas our de Bruijn nodes have k−1 letters. GAC labels the read overlap and the GA→AC edge. TG→GC would require TGC, absent from the observed graph even though the shared G is compatible.
Slides 24 and 26 — Context and altered windows
At k=4, the two read-derived chains meet at GAC. At k=5 the edge TGACC is missing. A middle G→T change in ATGAC alters all three 3-mers: ATT,TTA,TAC replace ATG,TGA,GAC. Changing the first base affects only one window. Ask which observations are independent.
Slides 31 and 32 — Support and unresolved sequence
The branch follow-ups are alternatives with the same initial counts. Independent, high-confidence, linked observations support retaining the minority alternative; copies of one low-confidence molecule leave its sequence uncertain. Linking evidence can order and orient contigs without filling their gap; the schematic gap has no specified length.
Slide 36 — N50 can increase after deletion
Removing 5 from [8,7,5] leaves total 15, halfway 7.5, N50 8, L50 1. Assume the deleted contig was genuine distinct target sequence when discussing lost completeness.
Slide 38 — Choose a spanning observation
GAC repeats the shared context. TGACC links T on its left and C on its right in one observation and supplies the missing k=5 edge. This supports the local adjacency; unique genomic placement and independent support still require checking.

Activity answers and facilitation

1. Same mean, different missing positions

Coverage lab: G = 12, L = 4, N = 3. Starts [0,4,8] give twelve depth-1 positions, mean 1×, breadth 100%. Starts [0,0,0] give four depth-3 positions and eight zeros, also mean 1× but breadth 4/12 = 33.3%. Both contain twelve read bases. Have students describe the depth profile before reading the summaries. Random sampling uses a finite linear target: end positions have fewer possible covering starts. Ask students to identify remaining zero-depth positions after increasing the read count.

2. Compare tied joins

Overlap workbench: ATGAC and GACCT overlap by GAC, producing ATGACCT. In the preset ATGA/TGAC/TGAT, extending ATGA gives two equally long three-base overlaps, with possible joined strings ATGAC and ATGAT. Let half the room try each choice. At minimum overlap 3, each can become stuck with an unjoined fragment. A greedy choice can fail to explain all observations; a smaller threshold allows additional candidate joins but supplies no new measurements. Reset before comparing branches.

The tool always uses the largest exact suffix–prefix overlap for each ordered pair. It compares all eligible next fragments from the selected source; it does not search every assembly or infer orientations. Full overlaps can collapse identical/contained-at-an-end fragments. Ask why merging two identical records leaves one sequence representation even when two records were observed.

3. Build and spell a path

Graph lab: ATGACCT at k = 3 gives five distinct edges and six nodes. Edges ATG, TGA, GAC, ACC, CCT follow AT→TG→GA→AC→CC→CT and spell ATGACCT. Start with AT and append G,A,C,C,T. For reads ATGAC and GACCT there are six observed windows but five distinct words: GAC occurs twice, all others once. Two reads can cover the same single genomic location.

Ask one student to choose the next edge and another to justify why its source matches the current node. Distinguish an edge choice from a node label. Explain that an Eulerian traversal uses each edge occurrence exactly once in a specified graph; real reconstruction additionally needs the right graph, occurrence information, and biological evidence. Our graph compresses repeated observations into support labels, so the restricted traversal does not consume support as extra genomic copies.

At k = 3, 4, and 5, each five-base read yields 3, 2, and 1 windows. Larger k retains more context but can reduce connectivity when reads do not contain the required bridging words. Have students count those windows before changing the control.

4. Two strings share an inventory

Reconstruction comparison: ATGATCAT and ATCATGAT contain the same six 3-mers once each: ATG,TGA,GAT,ATC,TCA,CAT. Their two cycles at AT are traversed in opposite orders. A graph cycle does not establish circular DNA. At k = 4, the inventories differ. The extra forward read GATC occurs only in the first candidate, at start 2. This resolves the choice between these two candidates under exact-read assumptions; it does not exclude every possible genome.

Unlike the read-support example, this experiment supplies the complete positional-window inventory of a candidate target, with positions hidden. State that this inventory contains every target window once. Sequencing coverage generally supplies unequal numbers of observations.

5. Compare branch evidence / 8 minutes

Branch evidence exercise: spend 2 minutes inspecting the figure and making an initial choice, 3 minutes comparing the alternative follow-ups, and 3 minutes revising the decision for a specified analysis goal. The k = 3 paths are AT→TG→GA→AA, spelling ATGAA, and AT→TC→CA→AA, spelling ATCAA. Edge labels are ATG/TGA/GAA and ATC/TCA/CAA. Twelve versus three counts mean invented read records spanning an entire alternative. Their molecular independence is initially unknown. This example is separate from the culture case.

Ask for a vote before revealing either follow-up. A combines low confidence at the distinguishing base, one independently established input molecule copied into three records, one orientation, and longer observations supporting the other path. Ask how an amplification error could produce three records with C, and how amplification of one genuine rare molecule could produce the same pattern. B supplies high-quality support from three independently established input molecules, both orientations after normalization, and longer linking context. Ask what sample information would distinguish an allele from a rare mixture component. The two follow-ups describe alternative datasets with the same initial count ratio.

Ask students to separate a representative consensus from a variation-preserving target. A consensus choice must document unresolved alternatives; preserving genuine variation may require retaining the minority branch. Ask which evidence changes the decision: quality at the discriminating base, validated independent molecules, strand support, or longer reads linking the same flanks. Students should explain how the reconstruction goal changes what they retain.

6. N50 is a length summary

N50 calculator: lengths [8,7,5] have total 20, halfway threshold 10, cumulative totals 8,15,20, N50 7 and L50 2. [12,8] gives N50 12 and L50 1 with the same total. A false join can increase N50. The calculation cannot see it. Ask students what read, linking, completeness, and sequence-consistency evidence they would request. N50 weights the threshold by assembled bases. Its denominator is the assembled total, so omitted target sequence is not counted.

Homework handoff and key

Open the week-specific Colab link, save a copy in Drive, use a Python 3 CPU runtime, and run setup first. If the repository copy is unavailable, use the local download with Colab’s File → Upload notebook. Work through the supplied tasks in order. Reference solutions use Colab’s collapsible forms; students should attempt each exercise before revealing or running them. Running a reference cell replaces that function in the current runtime. Keep the separate instructor edition for the complete worked analysis.

Core tasks cover kmer_counts, build_kmer_graph, spell_path for a supplied ordered node path, and n50. Students do not implement an Eulerian-path search. The supplied examples and expected results connect directly to class. Allow about 50 minutes of core work plus 10–40 minutes for setup, debugging, and written interpretation; optional exploration is additional.

Submission: completed notebook with the initial prediction, k-mer counts, graph edge/support table, spelled sequence, N50 comparison, and written interpretation. Accept clear printed tables; no plotting package is required. Answers and boundary conditions are in the machine-readable key and homework guide. Do not grade a unique traversal order where several valid orders exist.

Review after teaching

Check whether students distinguish depth from breadth, observed support from genomic copy count, and contiguity from correctness. Record time spent on graph vocabulary and on the Colab setup separately. If graph traversal consumes the session, keep the reconstruction comparison and N50 critique; defer implementation details to the supplied homework scaffolds.
