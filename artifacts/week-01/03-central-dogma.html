From coding DNA to amino acids

Predict what changes when one DNA letter changes. Then step through the codons: DNA → RNA → a sequence of amino acids.

The display uses a coding DNA strand written 5′→3′. Its RNA sequence has U in place of T; transcription uses the complementary DNA template. Decoding begins at the frame you select. The tool omits initiation, splicing, and regulation, so its output gives an amino-acid sequence without predicting RNA or protein abundance.

Enter 3–90 uppercase A/C/G/T letters. Spaces, lowercase letters, and unknown bases are reported; your input is never silently cleaned.

Each full RNA triplet is a codon. A stop codon ends decoding and adds no amino acid. Bases before the chosen frame and incomplete final triplets are not decoded. Grey tiles after a stop stay untranslated.

Explore the standard genetic code

Codons are RNA, read 5′→3′. Activate a codon to highlight other codons with the same amino-acid meaning (or the three stop signals). This standard code is not universal; some organisms and organelles use alternatives. AUG encodes methionine and commonly initiates translation, but initiation depends on context.
