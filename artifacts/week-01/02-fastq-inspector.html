Try trailing quality trimming

From the right end, remove consecutive bases with Q below the threshold. Stop at the first Q at or above it. Keep the sequence and quality slices aligned, then discard any read shorter than the minimum. Internal low-quality positions can remain.

Use minimum 6 for the eight-base lesson toy; set 50 for the practical’s 80-base dataset. This is an exercise policy, not a universal rule for biological data.

Phred calculator

Q = ASCII(character) − 33
p(error) = 10−Q/10
Expected errors in a read = Σ p(error at each position)

Adding 10 to Q divides the estimated error probability by 10: Q10 → 10%, Q20 → 1%, Q30 → 0.1%. These are estimates from the base caller, not proof that a particular base is correct.

Expected errors is a count, not the probability of an error somewhere in the read. It can exceed 1. Sum the per-base probabilities; converting a mean Q score back to a probability does not give their arithmetic mean. Summing expectations does not require independent errors.

Orange rows mark Q < 20. This threshold helps you find uncertain positions; choosing a threshold for an analysis also depends on how much sequence you need to retain. Quality scores alone cannot identify contamination or a mislabeled sample.
