From a pore signal to letters

A nanopore senses an electrical signal as DNA moves through it. A computer infers sequence from that signal. Predict what repeated DNA context and added noise do in this deliberately simplified demonstration.

Schematic, not instrument data. Here a five-base window selects an invented signal level, in arbitrary units. Real sensing context, current levels, movement, and basecalling depend on the chemistry and model; five bases is not a universal rule. The toy decoder treats each window independently. Its mistakes are not a measurement of real sequencing accuracy.

Toy calls compared with known input

The toy decoder selects the closest nominal level among all 1,024 possible five-base strings, then reports only its center letter. Dots at the first and last two positions are outside the toy's full-window coverage. Real basecallers use a richer signal/context model and infer the sequence jointly.
